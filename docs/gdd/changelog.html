7. Changelog ​

Every published version stays playable at its own URL. The landing page lists them all.

latest (main) Live ​

  • Blocks come out of the middle of the gate's hole: a finished block waits (and starts its drop to the belt) about 8 px lower and 4 px further left than before, right in the middle of the gate's hole under the spout. All variants.
  • x2 turns on sooner: it now turns on by itself as soon as all the picture's sand is swept (none left fixed), instead of once all of it has fallen off the picture, so the falling sand already runs at x2. All variants.
  • Nest boxes: the light ring shows on every colour: the ring on a nest box's top face was faint or gone on light colours (yellow, yellowgreen, white) because the recolouring scaled and capped each channel. Each pixel of the box art is now read as the art's face colour shaded and mixed with white, and made the same way from the nest's colour, with at least as much white as in the art (more for light colours), so outline, shading and the ring keep their contrast. A white nest's face is a little greyer so its ring still shows. All variants.
  • Variant 2: blocks roll on the belt: the blocks on the conveyor are moved by physics, as in the Loop Sort reference video: the belt drags them along by friction, they crowd and push each other (a glancing bump spins them), the rims hold them on the belt, a block that slides over the belt (landing, or pushed) rolls over and settles on a face, and round the belt's ends they turn with it. Round the belt's ends the belt grips less than it takes to turn them at speed, so blocks slide out against the outer rim and pile up there, as in the video; now and then the belt jolts a block across it (more often back toward the middle) and it tumbles over, bouncing off the rims, so blocks roll in and out across the belt instead of hugging the outer rim. Sand becomes blocks about 9 times faster (about 23 blocks, 370 grains, a second, as fast as variant 1's sand): new blocks land across the belt's width already riding at nearly its speed, a few at once, and up to 4 finished blocks may wait before the hole plugs. Blocks still hop into the front nest of their colour as they pass right over it on the bottom run. Variant 1 is unchanged.
  • Gameplay variant 2: no ants: a block on the belt's bottom run jumps into the front nest of its colour right under it (a short hop). A block is 16 grains (was 64), so a picture makes about 4 times as many blocks, and nests are 4 times as big (as many nests as variant 1). The conveyor holds 100 blocks, lying loose as glossy 3D cubes (a shiny highlight where the light hits, like jelly sweets) turned every which way, in two staggered lanes either side of the belt's middle, so they touch and overlap a little but never pile up (no holders; 20 small arrows ride round with the belt, showing the way it turns), like the Sort Blocks reference. Variants 1 and 3 are unchanged. Played through level_6 with the test bot as a quick check; not tuned. See Gameplay variants.
  • Gameplay variants: the Level Editor's Play button has an arrow beside it that opens a list of three gameplay variants (Phiên bản 1, 2, 3) to playtest the same levels with; 1 is the game as it is, 2 and 3 play exactly like it for now, ready for their own rule changes. The game gets the variant as ?variant=2 or ?variant=3 (plain links play variant 1) and shows it on the Tap to play screen. See Level Editor.
  • Sand reaches the frame's top corners: the game cut the top corner grain or two of every picture (and the Level Editor left them empty), following the rounded corners of the old frame art; the new frame art is already full width where the sand starts, so nothing is cut there now (the bottom corners, which do curve toward the funnel, still are). The corner grains the cut had emptied are filled with the colour next to them in the levels whose top row is sand (2, 3, 4, 5 and 13–20).
  • New level order and pictures from a Level Bank export (colony-flow-levels (12).json): the cherries (was level_11) open the game as level_1; levels 2–5 are new full-frame scenes with zoomed grains (rocket ×1.5, cat and castle ×1.4, sailboat ×1.25; 6,675 to 9,396 grains); the earlier levels 5–10 follow as levels 6–11, and levels 12–20 are unchanged. The game's order (GAME_ORDER) follows the names given in the editor; the Level Editor's Game chapter is put in the new order once, and the marketing reels still show the same pictures. The export's three unfinished (WIP) projects are not in the game. Not checked with the test bot.
  • Sand fills the frame from side to side: with the bigger grains a whole number of them no longer spanned the frame's inside (136 grains of 6 px are 818 px, the inside is 824), so a thin light blue strip (about 3 px, more at some grain zooms) showed between the sand and the white rim on both sides. Each grain is now drawn a little wider (under 1 % at the normal size, at most about 2 % zoomed) so the sand meets both sides exactly, in every level and at every zoom. The Level Editor already showed it so.
  • Grain zoom is a slider: Phóng to hạt in the Level Editor is a slider from 1× to 2× in steps of 0.05 (it was four choices). While you drag, its label shows the zoom, the frame it gives and, in red, how many grains it would cut; letting go applies it, as one undo step. The game takes any frame from 64 to 128 grains tall as a zoomed frame.
  • Fix: editing a grain-zoomed level: after Phóng to hạt (1.25× or 1.5×), recolouring, filling, painting or erasing the picture failed (the level's frame is a fractional number of cells there, and the game's cell grid could not be made); it works at every zoom now.
  • Grain zoom (Phóng to hạt): a level can make its grains 1.25, 1.5 or 2 times as big (Level Editor, Settings), so its picture grows on screen without more sand: the frame then holds fewer grains (2×: 68 × 64 of about 12 px). The picture keeps all its grains (centred; any sand past the smaller frame is cut, with a toast). The game knows a zoomed level by its picture's size and scales the sand's fall speed and the loose-island limit to the grain size, so they look the same on screen.
  • Level Editor: smaller brush: the brush goes down to 0 (a single grain); it started at 2 (a dot about 5 grains wide). The cursor ring fits round even a single grain.

Sand falls and breaks up the same on screen: falling speeds are scaled to the grain size, loose islands are 40 grains or fewer (was 48) or thinner than 3 (was 4), and the swipe still frees 38.5 px round the finger. Pictures saved for the old frame (in a browser's Level Bank or an exported file) are resampled the same way when opened or played. In the Level Editor the image generator's picture size is 8–16 cells (was 12–22). Recorded wins need making again. Not checked with the test bot.
