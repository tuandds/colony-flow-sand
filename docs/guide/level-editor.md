# Level Editor

The level editor runs in the browser: [open the Level Editor](https://tuandds.github.io/colony-flow-sand/demo/editor.html) (or the **Level Editor** button on the landing page). On the dev server: http://localhost:8080/editor.html. Its labels are in Vietnamese. It follows the team's level editor template: an **Editor** mode with four panels and an **Organizer** mode for chapter sets.

It turns an image into a sand picture, grain by grain, in the game's own colours, then builds nests that a recorded play wins. It uses the game's own code (`src/game/levels.ts`, `src/game/sim.ts`, `src/game/bot.ts`), so what it shows is what the game plays.

The game's Tap to play screen shows it next to the version. Variant 2 has no ants, 16-grain blocks and a loose belt of 100 (see [Core gameplay](../gdd/core-gameplay#gameplay-variants)); variant 3 has no conveyor: blocks drop into an open yard and lie about, and ants walk out to fetch them (`src/game/variant.ts`: `VARIANT`, `RULES`). The editor's own block and nest counts are variant 1's. |

The phone panel can be dragged by its title bar, resized from its corner, reloaded or opened in its own window; it always stays inside the window, drawn narrower when the window is too short for it. Each panel can be collapsed (–) and resized by dragging the bar between panels; double-click a bar to reset it.

## 1. Colour Bank

- **Active Palette**: the level's own colours, plus the eraser. Every colour in the picture is always in it. Tap or drag a Bank colour into the box to add one; drag one back onto the Bank to take it off (only if the picture doesn't use it). Keys `1`–`0` pick the first ten. Long-press or right-click a colour to edit it.
- **Bank** ("every colour a single-word key"): the 30 standard colours (`SAND_PALETTE` in `src/game/levels.ts`), named after Cube Blast's voxel materials (`Voxel_*` in VoxelMaterials.json): black, blue, brown, cyan, darkblue, darkgreen, green, lavender, lightgreen, lightorange, lightpink, lightyellow, lime, magenta, maroon, navy, olive, olivegreen, orange, orangered, pink, purple, red, skyblue, springgreen, teal, turquoise, white, yellow, yellowgreen. Each is the middle tone (key 0.5) of the material's shading ramp, because Voxel.shader colours the cubes from that ramp only (not from `_BaseColor`), so some differ from their names' usual look (blue is a violet blue, orange a golden yellow). The old colours are gone: a Bank saved before is replaced by the 30 once (colours added by hand go too). A picture in other colours (an older saved level or an imported file) is moved onto the standard colours when it is opened or played: each colour goes to the nearest standard one by CIEDE2000, nearest pairs first, never two of its colours onto one, so its blocks and nests stay the same. Each row has a handle (⠿, drag to reorder), a swatch and its name. Tap a colour to paint with it; long-press or right-click to edit it.
- **Edit colour**: change the name (one word, no spaces, unique) or the colour, take it off this level's Active Palette, or delete it. A colour a picture uses can't be deleted. A changed colour changes that sand in every level using it, from its next save.
- **＋ Add colour** adds a colour to the Bank (up to 52); **↺** resets the Bank to the 24 default colours. The Bank is kept in the browser (`cf_editor_bank`); each level saves its own colours, so the game shows them as they were when it was saved.

## 2. Sand Editor

- A summary line (`colours › blocks › grains`) and notes: no sand, more than 12 colours, more than 420 blocks, and whether the nests are checked. Each colour makes its grains ÷ 64 blocks, rounded up (its last block takes the leftover grains), and its row in Counts shows its real grain count; the game plays the picture exactly as drawn.
- **Counts**: blocks and grains per colour, after the game's exact-fit trim.
- **Recolour by drag**: drag a colour from the Bank (or the Active Palette) onto a colour's row in Counts, or onto the picture, and every grain of that colour (the one under the pointer, on the picture) becomes the new colour. To a colour the picture doesn't use yet, the nests stay with their colour renamed; into a colour it already uses, the two merge and the nests must be generated again. `Ctrl+Z` undoes it.
- **🖼️ Create sand picture from an image…** opens the generator (below). You can also drop an image anywhere on the editor or paste one (`Ctrl+V`).
- The picture is drawn as sand, one pixel per grain, with the in-game grain shading, inside a copy of the game's frame. The frame fills the panel's width; it changes size only when the panel or the window is resized, never while you paint (the panels keep room for their scrollbar). The canvas is exactly the frame's inside: a picture that touches its edge here touches the frame in the game. The frame's inside has rounded corners at the bottom (where it curves in toward the funnel); they show in the rim's colour, nothing can be painted or filled there, the image converter leaves them empty, and saving drops any sand an older picture has there (the game cuts it). The frame is the same in every level, 188 × 176 grains (22 cells tall, a little wider than tall; a full frame is exactly 517 blocks, so a picture that fills it loses no sand), so sand cells are the same size from level to level; a smaller picture sits centred in it. Every 8 × 8 grains is one cell (faint grid); every 64 grains of a colour make one block.
- Tools: ✏️ paint (`B`), 🧽 erase (`E`), 🪣 fill a region (`G`), 🔁 replace a whole colour (`R`), 💧 pick (`I`); right-click erases; brush size 0–24 grains (`[` `]`; 0 paints a single grain); `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` undo and redo.
- **Move the picture:** the ← ↑ ↓ → buttons beside undo move the whole picture 0.2 cell at a time inside the frame. Grains are whole, so the steps take turns of 2 and 1 grains (2, 1, 2, 1, 2): five presses move it exactly one cell (8 grains). Sand pushed past the frame's edge (or into its rounded corners) is cut off, and the strip left behind on the other side stays empty, so a picture that fills the frame can be moved too; a toast says how many grains were cut, and the nests must then be generated again. A move that cuts nothing keeps the nests (the picture's grains are the same); either way its recorded win is dropped and it needs checking again. `Ctrl+Z` undoes a move.
- **Frozen areas (❄️, `F`):** the Frozen section under the picture lists the level's frozen areas (see [Core gameplay](../gdd/core-gameplay#obstacle-frozen)). **＋ Thêm vùng** adds a 5 × 5 cell square in the middle of the picture. With the ❄️ tool, drag on the picture to draw a new area (a click without a drag makes a 5 × 5 cell area there), drag inside an area to move it, and drag a corner of the picked one (gold outline and handles) to resize it; hold Shift to keep it square (or a true circle). Click an area or its chip to pick it, then set its shape (▢ square or ◯ round), its count (1–999) and its width and height in cells (steps of 0.5); `Delete` or 🗑 removes it. Areas stay inside the frame and are at least 1 cell wide and tall. A new area takes the last shape and count you set. Changing an area drops the level's recorded win, and `Ctrl+Z` undoes it. Areas are saved with the level (`frozen`) and played in the playtest.
- **Settings**: name, difficulty (nest size, from very easy 6–10 blocks to very hard 12–18), **Phóng to hạt** (grain zoom, a slider from 1× to 2× in steps of 0.05; each grain gets that many times as big, so the picture grows without more grains: the frame then holds fewer grains, from 136 × 128 at 1× through 101 × 95 at 1.35× to 68 × 64 at 2×. While you drag, its label shows the zoom, the frame and, in red, how many grains it would cut; letting go applies it: the picture's sand is centred in the new frame and sand past its edge or in its rounded corners is cut, with a toast saying how many grains; with nothing cut the blocks and nests stay; one `Ctrl+Z` undoes it; making a picture from an image puts the level back at 1×), status (WIP / REVIEW / LIVE) and a WIP comment (500 characters), saved with the level.

### Generator

Settings: only the Active Palette's colours (optional), picture size (8–16 cells tall, 64–128 grains, and about 1.11 times as wide; the picture is placed centred in the frame at the game's fixed scale, so a smaller size makes a smaller picture, not bigger cells), how the image sits in the frame (fit the whole image, or fill the frame and crop), where the picture sits in the frame (the arrow buttons move it a cell, 8 grains, at a time, ⊙ puts it back in the middle, or drag the preview; it stops where its sand would leave the frame, so empty space around the picture doesn't count), most colours (2–12, the outline not counted), background removal (grains this close in CIELAB to the image's corner colour become empty; transparent pixels are always empty), speckle smoothing (passes of a 3 × 3 majority filter), and an optional dark outline 4 grains wide (off by default, so the picture keeps the image's own edge).

How it works: every grain takes the nearest palette colour in CIELAB, so "near" means "looks alike". While there are too many colours, it drops the colour whose loss changes the picture least, so a small but distinct detail (a red roof, a sun) is kept. Empty pockets inside the picture (6 grains or fewer, not reaching its edge) take the colour round them, so the frame doesn't show through as pale dots; background removal makes them where soft edge pixels are close to the background colour. Specks of one colour inside another (a patch of 6 grains or fewer, counting diagonal neighbours, so a thin diagonal line stays) take the colour most common round them; the image's soft edges make them, for example pink grains along a white and dark border, whose edge pixels are a middle grey nearest to pink. Loose specks smaller than 64 grains are removed, because the game would let them fall at once. The preview shows the result as sand; under it, **Tranh mới** gives its blocks (in all and per colour) and grains, and **Tranh hiện tại** the blocks and grains of the level's picture now, to compare with; **Apply** replaces the picture.

## 3. Nest Editor

- **Nest columns**: 2 to 6 (default 4). The game spreads the columns around the screen centre, closer together past 4. Changing the count drops the current nests, so generate them again.
- A summary line (`nests › blocks in nests / blocks in the picture`) and a chip per colour showing whether the nests hold exactly the picture's blocks.
- **🤖 Auto-generate nests** finds nests the level can be won with. It runs up to 4 attempts at once in web workers and keeps the first one that wins twice:
  1. **Recorded play** (8 tries). The bot plays the picture from the bottom up, digging colours an open nest wants first and waiting while the belt is busy. Each column always has a nest. When one is done, the next opens at once for the colour most common on the belt, or about to arrive, or lowest in the picture. A nest that already holds most of its minimum size closes early when the belt gets crowded. The nests and the swipes are saved with the level (`nests`, `moves`). The check replays the same swipes against those nests. The game's rules use a seeded random generator, so the replay plays out exactly like the recording, which proves a winning set of swipes exists.
  2. **Fixed orders**: `rows` (bottom-up) and `onion` (outside-in) with seeds 0–5, played by the test bot.
  3. If nothing wins, the same again with nests one step smaller.
- The log shows each attempt; a failed one says whether it failed while recording or while replaying, what the front nests still needed and what was on the belt.
- **Edit nests by hand**: click a nest to change its colour or size, move it up/down or to another column, or delete it; ＋ adds a nest to a column. Drag a nest onto another nest to swap the two, or onto a column's ＋ to move it to the end of that column. Hand edits remove the ✓ and the recorded swipes.
- **Advanced**: the fixed order and seed, and a button that drops recorded or hand-made nests.

## 4. Level Bank

- Chips filter the list by chapter set (ALL = the whole bank).
- **＋ New Level**, **＋ Chapter Set**, search by name, filters (status, difficulty, check) and undo for bank actions.
- Each row: number, drag handle (drag to reorder), preview, name, status, colours, blocks, check (✓ checked, ! changed since, – not checked), difficulty, version history (the last 10 saved versions of that level), duplicate, delete.
- **Save** keeps everything in the browser (`localStorage`: `cf_custom_levels`, `cf_custom_chapters`, plus history and backups); changed game levels also go to the game (see [Game levels](#game-levels)). **Play all** opens the game with `?custom=1`, which plays the bank instead of the built-in levels.
- **Export / Import** JSON; **Copy TS** copies the current level as a `LevelDef` to paste into `LEVELS` in `src/game/levels.ts`.

## Game levels

The game's 10 built-in levels are in the Level Bank too (ids `builtin-0` to `builtin-9`, chapter set **Game**, status LIVE). They are added at the top the first time the editor opens, and saved with the bank on the next **Save**. A level you delete from the bank afterwards is not added again.

- They are converted from the cell pictures in `src/game/levels.ts` (`builtinDefs`): the same grains and the same nests (`salt` is the level's number, as in the game). Their colours are moved onto the standard colours like any other picture.
- **Edits reach the game.** The game (`index.html`, without `?custom`) plays, for each built-in level, the newest saved copy: this browser's Level Bank, or `src/game/level-edits.json`. It reads them each time a level starts, so the game updates on its next level or restart.
- **On the dev server**, every Save also writes the built-in levels that play differently from the original (picture, nests, difficulty, order, seed, columns or name) to `src/game/level-edits.json`, one level per line. Commit that file and publish to put the changes in the online game. A level in the file that is newer than this browser's copy replaces it when the editor opens (after a `git pull`, say).
- On the published site the editor cannot write files: changes there reach only the game in the same browser.
- To bring a level back to the original, delete it from the bank and save (or use its version history).

## Organizer

- **Chapter Sets** (left): new, rename (with a description), duplicate, delete, and import a set from JSON (its levels are added to the bank). "All levels" shows the bank itself.
- **Chapter Builder** (right): the set's levels as a table with order, name (click to open in the Editor), preview, blocks, nests, colours, status, difficulty, nest check, Auto Play result and check result. Drag rows to reorder; status and difficulty can be changed in place.
- Buttons: select levels / select all / delete selected (from the set; in "All levels" they are deleted from the bank), add levels, undo, save, **Play chapter** (the set's levels in order in the phone panel), **Auto Play set** (replays every level's recorded win in the workers, or lets the bot play), **Check set** (no sand, nests that don't fit, unchecked nests, too many colours, too large, no name), export CSV and JSON, edit set, delete set.

## Level format

A custom level is a `LevelDef` with `sand` (the whole frame: `n` = 16 cells, `size` = 128 grains tall, `w` = 136 grains wide, or a grain-zoomed frame of fewer, bigger grains (any whole number of grains from 64 to 128 tall, as wide as frameGrains makes it, `n` its height / 8: 68 × 64 at 2×), which the game recognises by its size (pictures made for the older 188 × 176 frame, with smaller grains, are resampled to it when opened or played, and lose their hand-set nests; pictures made for the even older 186-wide frame fill the new one: their outer columns are doubled), see `FRAME_N` and `frameGrains` in `src/game/layout.ts`; a picture of another size is shown centred in the frame at the same scale; `data` run-length encoded row by row: a colour key followed by its run length, `.` = empty), `colors` (key → hex, from `SAND_PALETTE`), `chunk`, `order`, `seed`, `salt`, `cols` (nest columns, 2–6, default 4), `frozen` (optional frozen areas: `shape` `rect` or `circle`, `x`, `y`, `w`, `h` in picture grains, and `count`) and, when a recorded play found them, `nests` (each column front to back as `[colour, blocks]`) and `moves` (the winning swipes as `[time, points]`). The editor also stores `id`, `status`, `comment`, `verified` and `updated`; the game ignores them. The game uses `nests` as they are when they hold exactly the picture's blocks; when they are off by a little (up to 5% of the blocks) it resizes them to fit, and otherwise it falls back to `order` and `seed`. Levels from the first editor version (cell pictures in `rows`) are converted to grains when opened and need their nests generated again. Levels made at another size before the scale was fixed are placed centred in the 22-cell frame when opened (sand outside it is cut, and the check is marked as changed).
